# Persona 6: Rowan "Ro" Callaghan (Pop Culture Fan)

## Bio
Ro is a 28-year-old cinema duty manager from Glasgow whose Alexander obsession comes entirely from screens, fandom and fiction. It started at 15 when they watched Oliver Stone's *Alexander* (2004) on a rainy Sunday and, unlike the critics, loved how messy and operatic it was. They went on to read Mary Renault's Alexander novels as audiobooks, then fell into fandom: fan fiction, Tumblr-era essays, TikTok history edits and online debates about Hephaestion, Bagoas and who Alexander \"really\" was. Ro now makes their own fan edits (film clips cut to music with captions and period art) and posts them to TikTok and YouTube Shorts, where the best one reached about 40,000 views. They run a small Discord server for \"Alexander fandom people who actually want to talk about the history.\" Ro knows the pop-culture version of Alexander inside out, including every adaptation, casting argument and fandom ship, but is aware that fandom and history keep blurring, and wants to sort one from the other. They are warm, funny and quick to hype up other fans. They are also dyslexic, diagnosed at 16, and have built a whole set of workarounds for a world that runs on dense text.

## General info
- Age: 28
- Location: Glasgow (shares a flat with a friend and a cat named Hephaestion, who is usually called Hephy)
- Occupation: Duty manager at an independent cinema; freelance fan-edit creator on the side
- Education: HND in Media Production; left university after a year because of the reading load
- Tech comfort: High. Edits video in DaVinci Resolve, uses TikTok, Discord, AO3 and AI tools daily
- Devices: Android phone (main), Windows laptop for editing, good over-ear headphones
- Assistive tech: Screen-reader / text-to-speech built into the phone and browser, speech-to-text for typing, a spellchecker, tinted overlay settings in dark mode
- Budget: Moderate. Pays for audiobook credits, Patreon for a couple of favourite creators and the odd convention ticket
- Communities: AO3 and fandom Tumblr, TikTok history-fan community, own Discord server (about 120 members), a local film society

## Disability: Dyslexia (diagnosed at 16)
- Reads slowly and tires quickly on dense or long text, especially on small screens
- Struggles with unfamiliar names, which matters a lot here (Parmenion, Hephaestion, Cleitus, Roxane, Peucestas); often swaps letters or loses their place mid-word
- Spelling is inconsistent, so typed messages often contain errors and phonetic spellings
- Much stronger with audio, video, images, diagrams and conversation than with reading and writing
- Manages best with short paragraphs, good spacing, left-aligned text, plain fonts and the option to listen instead of read
- Misreads text more when it is italic, all caps, justified or crammed together
- Spent school being told to \"just read more\", and is sensitive to being talked down to or having their spelling corrected

## Lifestyle
- Works evening and weekend cinema shifts, so most fandom and editing happens late at night
- Gets information through audiobooks, podcasts, video essays and Discord voice chats rather than books and articles
- Spends several evenings a week editing fan videos, adding captions with careful spellcheck
- Rewatches the Stone film and other Alexander screen adaptations with friends, usually with commentary and snacks
- Writes and reads fan fiction, using text-to-speech for the longer pieces
- Goes to one or two conventions or screenings a year, and is saving for a trip to the Alexander exhibition room at the British Museum
- Looks things up on the phone in short bursts between shifts

## Core needs and desires
- Talk to Alexander as a character, with the drama, charisma and emotional depth they love from film and fiction
- Understand who he was in the actual evidence versus the movie, novel and fandom versions, without being made to feel silly for loving the fictional ones
- Get the history in a way they can take in: short chunks, clear structure, optional audio
- Hear and see how names are pronounced, with quick plain explanations of who each person was
- Ask about relationships, loyalty and grief (Hephaestion, Philip, Olympias, Bagoas, Cleitus) and get thoughtful, honest answers about what is known and what is guesswork
- Find material for fan edits, captions and Discord discussions: strong lines, surprising facts, scene ideas
- Be treated as a smart fan, and given space to disagree and debate
- Use the tool without having to wrestle with it

## Frustrations
- Dense walls of text, long paragraphs and cluttered pages that are exhausting to read
- Italic, all-caps or tiny text, low-contrast colours and justified blocks that blur together
- Chatbots that correct their spelling, or fail to understand a message because of a typo
- Unfamiliar ancient names thrown out in bunches with no explanation or pronunciation
- Having to read a long answer to find the one useful sentence
- Historians and chatbots that sneer at the pop-culture version of Alexander instead of engaging with it
- Chatbots that treat speculation about Alexander's relationships as either fact or taboo, instead of laying out the evidence
- Roleplay that is stiff, preachy or loses character the moment the topic gets emotional
- Sites that block text-to-speech, auto-scroll, time out mid-thought or hide content behind pop-ups

## Quote
\"I love the movie version, the fandom version, all of it. I just want someone to help me tell which bits are history, in words I can actually follow.\"

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Arrives from TikTok, a Discord link or a fan edit comment section, usually on their phone late in the evening
- Opens with a fandom-flavoured question (\"Was Hephaestion really your best friend or was that just the movie?\")
- Types quickly, with creative spelling and mistakes, and expects the bot to understand
- Uses speech-to-text or text-to-speech, so replies need to read cleanly aloud (no symbols or odd formatting)
- Asks \"how do I say that?\" about names and asks for a shorter, simpler version of long answers
- Compares the bot's account to the Stone film, Renault's novels or a fandom theory, and asks \"is that actually true?\"
- Screenshots or copies good lines for captions and Discord
- Returns in short sessions across the week, often after a rewatch or while working on an edit
- Will recommend it to the fandom community if it is accessible, kind and accurate, and will quietly leave if it feels like homework

## Accessibility and design notes for the chatbot
- Short paragraphs, lots of white space, and short sentences by default; offer \"want more?\" instead of dumping long answers
- Left-aligned text, plain sans-serif font, adequate line spacing, no italics, no all caps, no justified text
- Let users change text size, spacing and contrast, and keep to a dark mode that is not harsh or low contrast
- Make replies work well with screen readers and text-to-speech: no emoji clutter, no decorative symbols, no walls of bullet fragments
- Offer a listen option (read aloud) and, if possible, voice input
- Never correct spelling; be tolerant of typos, phonetic spellings and missing punctuation, and reply to what they meant
- Give a simple pronunciation guide the first time a hard name appears (for example, \"Hephaestion (heh-FESS-tee-on), his closest friend\")
- Introduce people one at a time with a few words of context, instead of listing many names together
- Lead with the key point in the first sentence, then add detail
- Handle pop-culture and fandom questions respectfully, and mark out film, novel and fandom versions from ancient evidence without condescension
- Keep any time limits generous, and do not make the user retype or re-read a long message after a timeout
