# Persona 2: Graham Whitcombe (History Enthusiast)
 
## Bio
Graham is a 63-year-old retired civil engineer from Shrewsbury who now spends most of his time on ancient and military history. His interest started at 11, when his father took him to see the Roman remains at Wroxeter, and it never left. Over four decades he has built a shelf of more than 300 history books, with a growing section on Alexander and the Hellenistic world. He has read Arrian, Plutarch, Curtius and Diodorus in translation and enjoys spotting where they disagree. He never went to university for history and is quietly sensitive about being "just an amateur", but he knows far more than most casual users and spots errors quickly. His wife Carol jokes that Alexander is "the other man in the marriage". He tried a general-purpose AI chatbot once, caught it inventing a quote, and has been sceptical ever since.
 
## General info
- Age: 63
- Location: Shrewsbury, Shropshire (lives with his wife, Carol; two grown children)
- Occupation: Retired civil engineer (35 years designing roads and bridges)
- Education: HNC, then chartered engineer status; no history degree
- Tech comfort: Moderate. Confident with email, forums and search; wary of "AI hype"
- Devices: Desktop PC for research, iPad for evening reading (rarely uses his phone for anything long)
- Accessibility: Wears reading glasses, so prefers larger text and good contrast
- Budget: Comfortable (pension); happy to pay for good books, lectures and battlefield tours
- Memberships: Local history society, an online Hellenistic-history forum, a classical-association reading group
## Lifestyle
- Reads 2-3 hours a day, usually in the morning with a pot of tea, and takes notes in a hardback notebook
- Active on a Hellenistic-history forum, where he often politely corrects other posters
- Gives occasional talks to his history society (his last was on Alexander's siege of Tyre)
- Visits battlefields and museums, and is planning a Greece and Turkey trip next spring to follow Alexander's route
- Watches long-form lectures and documentaries; switches off anything with invented dialogue
- Likes comparing translations, maps and campaign timelines side by side
- Plays the odd tabletop wargame with a friend, mostly ancient armies
- Walks the dog in the evening and mulls over whatever he read that day
## Core needs and desires
- Test his knowledge and explore "what if" questions about Alexander's decisions with a well-informed counterpart
- Hear Alexander's perspective on contested episodes (Cleitus, the Opis mutiny, the Hyphasis river) while knowing which source each claim comes from
- Get engineering-style reasoning on logistics, sieges, bridges and supply lines
- Find fresh angles and talking points for his society talks
- Have the bot admit uncertainty or gaps in the evidence rather than bluffing
- Be treated as a knowledgeable peer, not a beginner
- Feel a connection with the man he has studied for decades, without the history being cheapened
## Frustrations
- Chatbots that confidently invent quotes or details, which he catches immediately, then he stops trusting the whole tool
- Answers that flatten the evidence and ignore that Arrian, Plutarch, Curtius and Diodorus conflict
- Over-simplified "Alexander the genius conqueror" summaries
- Cartoonish, theatrical roleplay that treats history as costume drama
- Being talked down to, or buried in beginner basics he already knows
- Small text, cluttered pages and clumsy layouts that are hard to read on a tablet
- Bots that cave instantly when he challenges them, even when they were right
- Anachronisms, such as modern idioms or ideas put in an ancient mouth
## Quote
"If he's going to speak as Alexander, fine. But when he tells me something happened, I want to know whether that's Arrian, Plutarch or the chatbot making it up."
 
## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Opens with a deliberately obscure or loaded question to test accuracy (e.g. the exact circumstances of Cleitus's death)
- Asks for source attribution and checks answers against his own books
- Pushes back on claims and expects the bot to defend them or concede gracefully
- Writes longer, well-formed messages and has patience for detailed replies
- Tries to catch anachronisms or out-of-character slips
- Returns across several sessions, usually in the evening, and may copy material into his talk notes
- Likely to give detailed feedback if he finds an error, or to quietly leave if he feels misled
