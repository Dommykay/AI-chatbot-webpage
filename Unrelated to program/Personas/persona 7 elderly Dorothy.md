# Persona 7: Dorothy "Dot" Hargreaves (Tech-Nervous Newcomer)

## Bio
Dot is a 74-year-old retired school dinner lady from Norwich who has never really got on with computers. She raised two children, worked at the same primary school kitchen for 31 years, and was widowed two years ago when her husband Ron died. Ron was the history man of the house: he watched every documentary, kept a shelf of paperbacks about the ancient world, and talked about Alexander the Great at the dinner table for fifty years. Dot used to tune it out. Now she finds she misses it, and she has started reading his books and wondering what he found so fascinating.

Her grandson Callum (15) is doing a school project on Alexander and told her, \"Nan, you can just ask him yourself,\" then texted her a link to the chatbot. She has never used a chatbot and is not sure what one is. She thinks \"the internet\" is the blue icon on her tablet that her daughter set up. She is bright, funny and stubborn, and she is not stupid, but she has spent years being made to feel it by instructions written for people who already know how things work. She will give the page one honest go, partly out of curiosity and partly out of love for Ron. If it makes her feel foolish, she will close it and not come back.

## General info
- Age: 74
- Location: Norwich (lives alone in the house she and Ron bought in 1979; daughter Helen lives 20 minutes away, son Martin in Leeds)
- Occupation: Retired school kitchen assistant
- Education: Left school at 15; no formal history study
- Tech comfort: Very low. Can make a video call if someone has set up the shortcut, can read texts, and avoids almost everything else
- Devices: A tablet her daughter set up (main), a basic Android phone she mostly uses for calls and texts; no laptop
- Budget: Modest (state pension plus a small work pension); anxious about anything that might cost money or ask for card details
- Support: Helen helps on Sunday visits; Callum answers texts quickly; the local library runs a free \"tech help\" drop-in on Thursday mornings
- Communities: Church knitting group, the library's tech drop-in, a Tuesday coffee morning

## Lifestyle
- Up early, radio on, crossword and a pot of tea at the kitchen table
- Watches daytime TV and the evening news; has watched most of Ron's old history DVDs again since he died
- Reads one of his paperbacks a few pages at a time with a magnifier, and often falls asleep in the chair
- Knits for the church sale and for her grandchildren
- Phones Helen daily and speaks to Martin on Sundays
- Uses the tablet in short, nervous bursts, usually with a cup of tea nearby and the volume up
- Writes with a pen and paper; keeps a notebook of things Helen has shown her (\"press the little house\")

## Core needs and desires
- Feel closer to Ron by understanding what fascinated him about Alexander
- Have a patient person-like voice to ask the \"silly\" questions she never asked him, without being laughed at
- Get plain-English answers with no jargon, in short pieces she can follow
- Be able to help Callum with his project and surprise him with something she has learnt
- Know what she is doing at every step, with one clear thing to press and one clear sign that it worked
- Be sure she cannot break anything, spend money, or be tricked
- Be treated as an intelligent adult who has not been shown this before
- Have a gentle, unhurried experience that lets her go at her own pace

## Disability: Essential tremor (diagnosed at 68)
- Constant fine shaking in both hands, worse in the right (her writing hand); it grows worse when she is tired, anxious, cold or rushed, and when she holds her hands out to type or tap
- Struggles to tap small buttons and links accurately; often hits the wrong thing, or taps twice by accident
- Finds typing slow and error-prone. She uses one finger, and keys get pressed twice, skipped or mis-hit, so messages come out with repeated letters, wrong letters and stray punctuation (unlike a spelling difficulty, these are accidental hits, not wrong words)
- Pinch-to-zoom, drag, swipe and press-and-hold gestures are very difficult and sometimes impossible
- Can accidentally scroll, swipe back, close a pop-up or send a half-finished message when her hand shakes on the screen
- Has difficulty holding a tablet steady in one hand, so she usually props it on a stand or the table
- Finds time-limited actions stressful (timeouts, disappearing messages, buttons that vanish after a few seconds)
- Her tremor makes her self-conscious, and she is embarrassed when it causes mistakes in front of others
- Manages best with large, well-spaced buttons, few steps, forgiving taps and plenty of time; she has tried voice typing but finds it unreliable and does not trust it

## Frustrations
- Small links, tiny buttons and crowded screens where she keeps tapping the wrong thing
- Pop-ups, cookie banners, \"allow notifications\" boxes and anything she does not understand that blocks the page
- Not knowing whether something has worked, so she presses again and sends it twice
- Words like \"browser\", \"prompt\", \"tab\", \"login\", \"AI\" and \"chatbot\" that nobody has ever explained
- Being asked to create an account, remember a password or confirm by email
- Pages that move, reload or scroll away while she is reading
- Instructions that assume she already knows where things are (\"just click the hamburger menu\")
- Accidentally closing the page and losing the whole conversation, with no way back
- Being rushed or corrected, or made to feel like a nuisance for asking something basic
- Worry that it is a scam, that it records her, or that she will be charged
- Answers that run on for a long time, since she loses her place and her hands tire

## Quote
\"I'm not clever with these things, love. If it'll just tell me about Alexander the way Ron used to, slowly, and not make a fuss when I press the wrong thing, I'll be right as rain.\"

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Arrives from a text link from Callum or Helen, or after being shown the page at the library drop-in, usually on her tablet in the afternoon or early evening, sitting at the kitchen table
- Opens by addressing it politely and formally, as she would write a letter (\"Hello Alexander, my name is Dot and I'm not very good at this\")
- Types slowly with one finger and gets many accidental repeated or wrong letters; may send a message by mistake before she has finished it
- Sends the same message twice, or taps the send button repeatedly, if nothing seems to happen
- Asks things like \"Can you hear me?\", \"Is this a real person?\", \"Is this going to cost anything?\", \"Am I doing this right?\"
- Asks plain, personal questions (\"Were you frightened?\", \"Did your mother love you?\", \"What was your horse called?\") before any facts
- Mentions Ron and what he used to say about Alexander, and may want to talk for a while
- May stop mid-conversation if she is distracted by the kettle, the phone or an accidental tap, then come back later and not know where she was
- Visits in short sessions of 10 to 15 minutes, a few times a week, often after Callum asks how it is going
- Will tell Helen, her church group and the library group about it if it is gentle and clear, and will quietly give up if it is confusing or fiddly

## Accessibility and design notes for the chatbot
- Make the first screen as simple as possible: one clear box to type in, one large Send button, and one line explaining what to do (\"Type a question below, then press Send\")
- Use large tap targets with generous spacing, so a shaky hand is less likely to hit the wrong thing; avoid small links, icon-only buttons and anything placed close to the screen edge
- Prevent accidental double-sends: disable Send after one tap, and show clear \"Alexander is thinking...\" feedback so she knows it worked
- Let her review or edit a message before it is sent, and make it easy to undo or fix a mistaken send
- Do not use timeouts, disappearing elements, auto-advancing carousels or anything that needs to be done quickly
- Avoid gestures such as drag, swipe, pinch or press-and-hold; every action should be a single, simple tap
- Be forgiving of accidental extra letters, repeated words and stray punctuation, and answer what she meant, without pointing out mistakes
- Keep the conversation safe from accidental loss: save it automatically, warn before leaving the page, and bring her back to where she was if she reloads
- Support voice input if possible, but keep it optional, and make typing work well on its own
- Avoid sign-ups, passwords, cookie walls and notification prompts; say plainly that it is free and does not need any personal details
- Write in plain English with no jargon and no screen-talk; if something must be named, explain it in a few words (\"the box at the bottom\")
- Short answers, short paragraphs, lots of white space, with the main point first; offer \"Would you like to hear more?\" rather than long replies
- Provide a larger text option and high contrast, and use a plain, readable font
- Alexander should be warm, patient and gently good-humoured, and should welcome her \"silly\" questions
- Reassure at the start, in character, that she cannot break anything and there is no rush
- Handle personal or emotional moments (such as her mentioning Ron) with kindness, without dwelling or becoming over-sentimental
- Keep clear which parts are history and which are legend, in simple terms, because she will likely repeat what she learns to her family
