# Persona 3: Dan Reyes (Casual History Enthusiast)

## Bio
Dan is a 34-year-old logistics coordinator from Manchester who got hooked on history through podcasts and strategy games rather than books or school. He fell for Alexander after binge-listening to a long-form podcast series on the Macedonian campaigns during a run of night shifts, then bought a copy of Total War: Rome II Alexander mode and has been chasing the \"real story\" ever since. He has read two popular Alexander biographies and watched most of what YouTube offers, but he has never read the ancient sources and does not claim to know them. He is enthusiastic, chatty and happy to be corrected, but he has little patience for anything that feels like homework. He is drawn to the chatbot because it sounds like fun: he wants to hear the man himself tell the stories he has only heard secondhand.

## General info
- Age: 34
- Location: Manchester (rents a flat with his partner, Jess)
- Occupation: Logistics coordinator at a regional distribution company (shift work, some nights)
- Education: A-levels, then a vocational logistics qualification; no history study since school
- Tech comfort: High. Lives on his phone, comfortable with apps, Discord and AI tools
- Devices: Android phone (main), gaming PC at home; occasionally a work laptop on breaks
- Budget: Moderate. Will pay for a game, a podcast subscription or a museum day out, but not much for books
- Communities: A history-gaming Discord server, a couple of history subreddits, a monthly pub quiz team

## Lifestyle
- Listens to history podcasts on his commute and during night shifts, often 1-2 hours a day
- Plays Total War and Crusader Kings in the evenings, sometimes streaming with friends
- Reads in snatches on his phone (Wikipedia rabbit holes, Reddit threads, YouTube comments)
- Visits a museum or heritage site a few times a year; the British Museum's Alexander coins were a highlight
- Shares \"did you know?\" facts with Jess and his friends, sometimes getting them slightly wrong
- Wants to learn more but struggles to find time and structure around shift patterns
- Likes a bit of banter and humour in how things are explained

## Core needs and desires
- Hear the best stories about Alexander told with personality (Gordian knot, Bucephalus, the Persian court, Gaugamela) without wading through academic prose
- Get quick, clear answers in short chunks he can read in a spare ten minutes
- Learn what is solid history and what is legend, in plain terms, without feeling stupid for asking
- Ask playful \"what if\" and game-style questions (\"how would you have handled Gaugamela in Total War?\")
- Pick up facts and stories he can share with friends and look knowledgeable
- Be guided to the next thing worth exploring, such as a book, podcast episode or place to visit
- Feel a bit of the thrill of talking to a legendary figure

## Frustrations
- Dry, textbook-style answers that read like an encyclopaedia entry
- Long unbroken blocks of text, especially on a small phone screen
- Roleplay that is stiff or overly formal, so the character feels like a lecture
- Not knowing which \"facts\" are myth, since podcasts and games blur the line
- Chatbots that overload him with names, dates and jargon without explanation
- Being corrected in a snooty or condescending way
- Slow, clunky or gimmicky web pages that interrupt the flow
- Bots that forget what was said a few messages ago

## Quote
\"I just want to chat with Alexander like he's a mate down the pub, but I don't want to walk away repeating something that turns out to be made up.\"

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Arrives from a podcast, game or Reddit link and starts with something fun (\"Did you really tame Bucephalus as a kid?\")
- Sends short, casual messages, often typed on his phone with typos
- Jumps between topics, with game and pop-culture comparisons mixed in
- Asks \"is that actually true?\" when something sounds too good
- Screenshots good replies to share in Discord or with Jess
- Visits in short sessions on breaks or commutes, and may return after a podcast episode sparks a new question
- Likely to leave if replies are slow, long-winded or dull, and to recommend it to friends if it is fun and accurate
