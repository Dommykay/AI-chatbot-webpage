# Persona 4: Priya Nair (Museum Goer)

## Bio
Priya is a 41-year-old primary school teacher from Bristol who treats museums as her main way of switching off and refilling her curiosity. She grew up going to the Bristol Museum on Saturday mornings with her dad, and now takes her own kids (Anika, 9, and Dev, 6) on most school holidays. She is a paying member of two museums and keeps a running list of exhibitions she wants to see. She has no specialist knowledge of Alexander the Great beyond school and a few exhibition panels, but she is a confident learner who likes context: who made this object, why it mattered, and what people argued about. She came to the chatbot after seeing an Alexander coin and bust display at a museum, where a QR code invited her to \"ask Alexander\". She likes the idea of hearing a voice behind the objects, but she is wary of anything that feels like a gimmick or that she cannot trust to be accurate, especially if she might repeat it to children.

## General info
- Age: 41
- Location: Bristol (lives with her husband, Rohan, and their two children)
- Occupation: Year 4 primary school teacher
- Education: BA English Literature, PGCE; no formal history study since school
- Tech comfort: Moderate to high. Comfortable with apps, QR codes, museum audio guides and chatbots, though she is not an early adopter
- Devices: iPhone (main, especially in museums), family iPad at home, school laptop
- Budget: Moderate. Pays for museum memberships and family days out, watches ticket prices, and likes free-entry galleries
- Memberships and habits: Two museum memberships, a local arts-and-culture newsletter, and a shared \"days out\" list with her sister

## Lifestyle
- Visits a museum or gallery about once or twice a month, usually at weekends or during school holidays
- Usually has a short window (60 to 90 minutes) before the kids tire, so she plans her route and picks highlights
- Takes photos of object labels and information panels to read later, then rarely does
- Uses audio guides and QR codes if they are quick and work on her phone, and gives up if they are slow or need a login
- Reads in the evenings once the kids are asleep: a mix of novels, history podcasts and long articles
- Likes to bring what she learns back to her classroom, so she is always looking for a good story or surprising fact
- Often visits with the kids, so she wants things she can explain simply and share in the moment

## Core needs and desires
- Understand the objects and exhibits in front of her, with the story behind them (who, why, what it meant)
- Get short, clear answers she can absorb in a couple of minutes while standing in a gallery or sitting in a cafe
- Hear Alexander's voice with warmth and personality, to make the past feel human and not a list of dates
- Learn what is well documented and what is legend, so she can pass on accurate information to her children and pupils
- Get ideas for follow-up questions, related objects and what to look at next in the museum
- Find child-friendly angles: stories her kids would enjoy, such as Bucephalus, the Gordian knot and the long march east
- Leave a visit feeling she has learnt something new and had a rewarding day out

## Frustrations
- Museum labels that are too small, too academic or assume prior knowledge
- Exhibit tech that feels gimmicky, slow, or breaks when the gallery has poor signal
- Long walls of text, especially on a phone while standing up
- Chatbots that pretend to be certain when the evidence is thin, particularly when she might repeat the answer to children
- Character roleplay that turns violent or glorifying about conquest with no context, which she finds uncomfortable around kids
- Having to create an account or fill in forms before she can use anything
- Answers that ignore the object or exhibition she is actually looking at
- Feeling talked down to for not being an expert

## Quote
\"I don't need a lecture. I want someone to tell me why this coin matters, in a way I can pass on to my kids, and be straight with me about what we actually know.\"

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Arrives by scanning a QR code or following a link on a museum display, often on her phone mid-visit
- Starts with a question about something she can see (\"What is this coin telling us about you?\", \"Why does your statue look like that?\")
- Types short, practical messages, and may read answers aloud to her children
- Asks follow-ups like \"is that actually true?\", \"how would you explain that to a nine-year-old?\" and \"what else should I look at here?\"
- Expects quick replies, and may close the chat if it is slow, long-winded or needs a sign-up
- Returns in the evening or on a later visit with questions inspired by the day out
- May share a good answer with her sister or use it in a lesson, and is likely to recommend it to other parents if it is accurate, engaging and family-friendly
