# Persona 1: Maya Okafor (Student)

## Bio
Maya is a 19-year-old first-year History and Politics student at the University of Leeds. She grew up in Coventry, the eldest of three, and is the first in her family to go to university. She picked history because of a Year 9 teacher who made the ancient world feel like gossip about real people. She is curious and quick, but she worries she is "behind" on the reading compared with students from selective schools.

## General info
- Age: 19
- Location: Leeds (student house share, 4 housemates)
- Course: BA History and Politics, Year 1
- Tech comfort: High on her phone, moderate on laptop; uses ChatGPT, YouTube explainers and Quizlet
- Devices: iPhone (most use), 5-year-old Windows laptop
- Budget: Tight (maintenance loan plus 12 hrs/week in a café)

## Lifestyle
- Studies in short bursts between lectures, the bus and late evenings
- Watches history YouTube and documentaries instead of reading when tired
- Plays Civilization VI and Assassin's Creed with housemates
- Unwinds with the university history society (pub quiz night)
- Often reads on her phone in bed, so mobile experience matters

## Core needs and desires
- Understand Alexander's campaigns, motives and legacy well enough to write essays with confidence
- Quickly check facts and get different interpretations (hero, tyrant, strategist)
- Make revision feel like a conversation, not a textbook
- Practise essay arguments by being challenged
- Feel the past is alive, with a vivid voice and personality

## Frustrations
- Generic chatbots give bland, wikipedia-style answers with no personality
- Not knowing whether an answer is historically accurate or invented for effect
- Walls of text that are hard to skim on a phone
- Chatbots that agree with everything she says instead of pushing back
- Gimmicks that get in the way of actually learning

## Quote
"I want to argue with Alexander, not read about him. But I also need to know that what he says is actually true."

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Opens with a casual question ("Did you really cut the Gordian knot?")
- Tests the bot with trick questions and asks for sources
- Tries to get it out of character to see if it stays consistent
- Uses it the night before an essay deadline
