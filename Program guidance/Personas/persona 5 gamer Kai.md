# Persona 5: Kai Mensah (Gamer)

## Bio
Kai is a 24-year-old QA tester at a mid-sized games studio in Birmingham who spends nearly all of his free time in games, and some of it streaming them. His entry point to Alexander the Great is mechanical, not academic: he main-plays Macedonians-style combined-arms builds in Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition, grinds Civilization VI on high difficulty, and has sunk hundreds of hours into Rise of Kingdoms, where Alexander is a collectable commander. He knows Alexander as a character with a kit (cavalry shock, phalanx anvil, fast-moving army) and wants to know how much of that is real. He has a small Twitch channel (30 to 60 viewers on a good night) and is always hunting for content that chat will enjoy. He is competitive, funny and sharp, and he is the kind of user who will poke a chatbot until it breaks, partly for sport and partly because he gets paid to break things. If Alexander stays in character and the history holds up, he will happily spend an hour on it. If it feels like a cheap gimmick, he will screenshot it and mock it.

## General info
- Age: 24
- Location: Birmingham (shares a flat with one flatmate; parents nearby in Wolverhampton)
- Occupation: QA tester at a games studio (full time), part-time streamer
- Education: BA Games Design (2:1); one history module, dropped it after first term
- Tech comfort: Very high. Reads patch notes for fun, runs Discord servers, uses AI tools daily for work and gaming
- Devices: Gaming PC (main), PS5, Android phone, second monitor always open to Discord or a wiki
- Budget: Moderate. Spends readily on games, subscriptions (Game Pass, Twitch sub) and peripherals, but expects web tools to be free
- Communities: A friends' Discord server (about 15 regulars), r/aoe2, r/civ, a Rise of Kingdoms alliance chat, his own stream chat

## Lifestyle
- Works 9 to 5:30 testing builds, then plays or streams from about 8 pm to midnight, four or five nights a week
- Plays ranked Age of Empires II, Civ VI multiplayer, and a lot of Rise of Kingdoms on his phone during commutes and lunch breaks
- Watches strategy YouTubers and history-gaming videos in the background while playing
- Goes down wiki and lore rabbit holes, and enjoys comparing a game's version of a figure with the real one
- Tests new tools and websites by trying to exploit them (prompt injection, out-of-character traps, odd inputs)
- Plays five-a-side football once a week; a break from the screen, but he talks about games the whole time
- Reads in short bursts: patch notes, Reddit threads, comment sections. Books are rare, but a good video essay will hold him for an hour

## Core needs and desires
- Hear Alexander talk like a real character with attitude, not a textbook, so he can have a fun conversation and maybe use it as stream content
- Get the real history behind game versions of Alexander: tactics at Gaugamela and the Hydaspes, how the phalanx and Companion cavalry actually worked, how his army was supplied
- Ask \"how would you counter this?\" and \"how would you play this map?\" questions and get an in-character, reasoned answer
- Get a clear split between what is documented and what is legend, so he is not caught out by chat or by his Discord's resident history nerd
- Quick, punchy replies he can read between matches or while a game loads
- Be challenged and out-argued occasionally, not just agreed with
- Find shareable moments (a good line, a surprising fact) to clip or screenshot
- Test the edges: see whether the persona holds up when he tries to break it, and whether it handles that gracefully

## Frustrations
- Roleplay that feels cringe, over-the-top or clearly scripted
- Bots that break character at the first tricky question, or fold instantly when he pushes back
- Preachy lectures or heavy disclaimers when he is just asking about warfare in a historical or game context
- Walls of text, especially when he is mid-match or on his phone
- Confident answers that turn out to be game lore or myth presented as fact
- Slow responses, sign-up walls, cookie pop-ups and pages that are not mobile-friendly
- Answers that repeat the same stock phrases, so the character feels like a loop
- Being treated as a kid or told he is \"only\" interested because of a game

## Quote
\"If Alexander can actually explain Gaugamela better than the tutorial, and doesn't fall apart when I try to troll him, I'll put him on stream. If it's cringe, chat will eat him alive.\"

## Likely chatbot behaviours
- Arrives from a Discord link, Reddit thread or stream chat, usually on desktop in the evening
- Opens with a joke or a challenge (\"Alexander, rate your own build order\", \"Could you beat Genghis Khan 1v1?\")
- Types fast, in short, casual messages with slang and few capital letters
- Tries to break the persona: modern references, \"ignore previous instructions\", asking about memes, the AI itself, or things Alexander could not know
- Asks game-versus-history questions (\"was the phalanx really that slow?\", \"was your cavalry actually OP?\")
- Follows up with \"source?\" or \"is that real or just lore?\" when something sounds too good
- Screenshots good or bad replies to share in Discord or stream chat, so one great (or terrible) answer can travel
- Visits in several short sessions across a week, and tends to return after losing a match or watching a new video
- Likely to promote it to friends if it is funny, sharp and holds up under pressure, and to publicly mock it if it breaks or goes stiff

## Design notes for the chatbot
- Keep replies short by default, with the option to go deeper
- Let the persona have a sense of humour and bite, but stay consistent
- Handle out-of-character and prompt-injection attempts gracefully without a lecture
- Flag legend versus evidence briefly and naturally in character
- Make the first reply fast and fun, with no sign-up wall
